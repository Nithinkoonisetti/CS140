Hawaiian Culture and Traditions

Hawaiian culture is living and evolving. Language, storytelling, music, hula, and relationships to place remain important to many communities. Learn more through the Bernice Pauahi Bishop Museum.

Language and Place

‘Ōlelo Hawai‘i (the Hawaiian language) is central to many place names, songs, and cultural practices. Pronunciation and diacritical marks such as the ‘okina and kahakō matter.

Explore language resources from University of Hawai‘i Hawaiian language resources.

Hula, Storytelling, and Respect

Hula can convey history, genealogy, and stories through movement and chant. It is more than entertainment. Visitors should ask before photographing ceremonies or people and follow site-specific guidance.

Explore Hawaiian collections and cultural education at the Bishop Museum.

How to Be a Respectful Guest

Support locally owned businesses, respect private property, stay on designated trails, and follow instructions at cultural sites. The Mālama Hawai‘i program introduces the idea of caring for the place you visit.
